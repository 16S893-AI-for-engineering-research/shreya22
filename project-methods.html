Methods

The four tasks from the proposal, expanded into what actually has to be built and in what order.

Task 1 — Demand and network baseline

Construct a present-day US baseline: origin–destination passenger demand, scheduled operations by route, and aircraft/engine assignment per flight. This is the reference case every alternative network is compared against.

Output: a reproducible baseline network with fuel burn attributed per flight leg.

Task 2 — Re-optimise network structure

Hold total demand fixed and re-allocate it across a spectrum of network structures, from hub-consolidated to fully point-to-point. For each structure, solve for the fuel-minimising assignment of aircraft and frequencies subject to capacity and connectivity constraints.

Output: a family of candidate networks indexed by how hub-centric they are.

Task 3 — Emissions to impacts

Two parallel translations from fuel burn:

Climate: CO₂ from fuel burn, plus a note on non-CO₂ forcing where the network change affects cruise altitude or routing.

Air quality: spatially resolved NOₓ and nvPM emissions, separated into landing/takeoff and cruise phases, mapped to PM₂.₅ and ozone exposure and then to health burden weighted by the population actually exposed.

Task 4 — Map the trade-off

Place every candidate network on a climate-versus-health plane and identify where the two objectives disagree, how large the disagreement is, and which network structures are Pareto-efficient.

Then stress-test it: re-run the comparison under alternative assumptions for every proxy used in place of restricted data, and report which conclusions survive and which do not.

Deliberate scope limits

US domestic only. Passenger operations only. Relative comparisons between network structures rather than absolute fuel accounting. Demand is treated as fixed rather than responding to changes in fares or itinerary quality.
