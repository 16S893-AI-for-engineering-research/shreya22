Data

What is open, what is restricted, and exactly which assumption stands in for each thing I cannot see.

Open inputs

Available and usable directly

Scheduled flights and seat counts, airport-level passenger totals, aircraft and engine type assignments, certified engine emission indices, and gridded population and meteorological fields.

Restricted inputs

Commercial or proprietary

Actual fuel uplift per flight, per-flight load factors, airline cost and yield data, manufacturer performance decks, and full-resolution real trajectories at national scale.

The bridge

Proxies, stated as assumptions

Fuel burn comes from open performance models rather than measured uplift. Load factors come from published averages by route type rather than per flight. Trajectories are idealised climb–cruise–descent profiles rather than observed tracks.

Each of these is a declared assumption with an associated sensitivity test, and the conclusions are stated as relative differences between network structures rather than absolute totals.

Why this is defensible

The question is comparative: does the fuel-optimal network differ from the health-optimal one? Systematic bias in a fuel model affects both candidate networks in the same direction, so the sign and rough magnitude of the difference is far more robust than either absolute number. Where that is not true, the sensitivity analysis should expose it.
